1. Exact algebra

Answer: C — 34/15
Squaring once gives 2x + 2√(x2 − 1) = 3x + 1, so 2√(x2 − 1) = x + 1. Squaring again: 4(x2 − 1) = (x + 1)2. Hence 3x2 − 2x − 5 = 0, giving x = 5/3 (the other root is outside the domain). Therefore x + 1/x = 5/3 + 3/5 = 34/15.

2. Roots and parameters

Answer: D — 8
Let the smaller positive root be r; the other is 2r. Expanding (x − r)(x − 2r) gives x2 − 3rx + 2r2. Comparing coefficients, 3r = k + 1 and 2r2 = 2k + 2, so r2 = k + 1. Thus r2 = 3r. Since r > 0, r = 3, so k + 1 = 9 and k = 8.

3. Circle geometry

Answer: C — 64/3
Let M be the midpoint of chord AB. Then AM = 4 and OM = 3 from the 3–4–5 triangle. Since equal tangents give symmetry, O, M, T are collinear. Right triangles OMA and OAT are similar, so OT/OA = OA/OM. Hence OT = 25/3 and TM = 25/3 − 3 = 16/3. Area = ½ × 8 × 16/3 = 64/3.

4. Trigonometric structure

Answer: D — π
Rearranging and factorising gives cos x(2 sin 2x − 1) = 0. Since sin x > 0, the permitted interval is 0 < x < π. The cosine factor gives x = π/2. The other factor gives 2x = π/6 or 5π/6, so x = π/12 or 5π/12. Their sum is π. No trigonometric addition formula is needed.

5. Composite functions

Answer: C — 5
Put y = f(x). Then f(y) = 2 gives y3 − 3y − 2 = 0 = (y − 2)(y + 1)2. So f(x) is either 2 or −1.

f(x) = 2 gives (x − 2)(x + 1)2 = 0: two distinct roots. For f(x) = −1, x3 − 3x + 1 = 0. Write g(x) = x3 − 3x + 1. Its derivative is g′(x) = 3x2 − 3: g increases for x < −1, decreases for −1 < x < 1, and increases for x > 1. Also g(−1) = 3 and g(1) = −1. Together with the cubic's end behaviour, this gives exactly one root in each of these three intervals. None can also satisfy f(x) = 2. Total: 2 + 3 = 5.

6. Counting with cases

Answer: D — 876
The final digit is 2, 4 or 6.

If the final digit is 2: there are 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 = 360 arrangements for the first four positions. The bad cases have 1 immediately before the final 2, giving 5 × 4 × 3 = 60. So this case contributes 300.

If the final digit is 4 or 6: for either choice there are again 360 arrangements. To count bad arrangements, place 1 and 2 as an adjacent block among the first four: choose the other two digits in (4 × 3)/2 = 6 ways, order the three objects in 3 × 2 × 1 = 6 ways, and order 1,2 inside the block in 2 ways. This gives 6 × 6 × 2 = 72 bad arrangements. So each of these two final digits contributes 360 − 72 = 288.

Total = 300 + 288 + 288 = 876.

7. Coordinate geometry + tangent

Answer: C — 10
Write the circle as x2 + y2 + Dx + Ey = 0. Using B = (8,0) gives D = −8. Using C = (2,6) then gives E = −4. So the centre is (4,2).

The radius from (4,2) to (2,6) has slope −2, hence the tangent at C has slope 1/2. Its equation is y − 6 = ½(x − 2). Setting y = 0 gives x = −10. Thus T = (−10,0), so AT = 10.

8. Necessary and sufficient

Answer: C — P is necessary and sufficient for Q
If Q holds, n − 1 and n + 1 are consecutive even integers: one is divisible by 4 and the other by 2. Their product n2 − 1 is therefore divisible by 8. Among the three consecutive integers n − 1, n, n + 1, one is divisible by 3. Since n is not, the product (n − 1)(n + 1) is divisible by 3. Because 8 and 3 have no common factor greater than 1, the product is divisible by 24. Thus Q implies P.

Conversely, if n were even, n2 − 1 would be odd. If n were divisible by 3, n2 − 1 would not be divisible by 3. Either case contradicts P. Thus P implies Q as well.

9. Counterexample reasoning

Answer: B — (√2, −√2)
Here a + b = 0 and ab = −2, both rational, while neither √2 nor −√2 is rational. One valid counterexample is enough to disprove the universal claim. Option E does not work: its product is −√6, which is irrational.

10. Repeated-root / intersection reasoning

Answer: C — 0
Intersections satisfy x3 − (m + 3)x − 2 = 0. Exactly two distinct intersections means this cubic has two distinct real roots, with one repeated. Write it as (x − t)2(x − u). Comparing the coefficients of x2 gives u = −2t. Expanding now gives x3 − 3t2x + 2t3.

Comparing constants gives 2t3 = −2, so t = −1. Comparing the coefficients of x gives m + 3 = 3t2 = 3, hence m = 0. The factorisation (x − 2)(x + 1)2 confirms exactly two distinct intersections.
