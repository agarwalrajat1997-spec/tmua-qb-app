Question 14 · Answer E

Let T(n) count tilings of a 3-by-n strip. Its first column is either all unit squares, or starts a 2-by-2 square in one of two positions. In the second case the two cells beside the square must be unit squares. Thus T(n) = T(n−1) + 2T(n−2). Starting with T(0) = T(1) = 1 gives 3, 5, 11, 21, 43, 85, 171. The required value is 171.

Question 15 · Answer A

Question 18 · Answer A

Factor n²⁰¹⁵ − n as n(n²⁰¹⁴ − 1). Its two factors are coprime, so if their product is square and positive, each is a square. For n > 1, the second factor is exactly one below (n¹⁰⁰⁷)² and above the preceding square. It cannot be square. The value n = 1 works because 0 = 0²; there is exactly one.

Question 20 · Answer C

Twenty-four elves receive two gifts and sixteen receive one. Choose the 24 elves, then order all gifts and ignore the order inside each pair. N = C(40,24) × 64! / 2²⁴. The exponent of 2 in 64! is 63. In C(40,24) it is 38 − 22 − 15 = 1, by summing multiples of 2, 4, 8, … in the three factorials. The exponent in N is 40; a base-4 zero uses two factors of 2, giving 20.

Question 22 · Answer D

The 4,029 replies must be the integers 0 to 4,028. The person who does not know 4,028 people knows only their spouse; the person who knows everyone must be that spouse. Remove this pair: among those left, each unknown-count falls by one. Repeating pairs off the opposite extremes, leaving the man’s wife with the middle original unknown-count, 2,014. She knows 4,029 − 2,014 = 2,015 people.

Question 23 · Answer E

Question 24 · Answer E

Let a(n) = floor(n²/2015). Through n = 1008, consecutive unrounded terms differ by at most 1 because (n+1)² − n² = 2n+1. The floors therefore take every integer from 0 to floor(1008²/2015) = 504: 505 values. Each of the remaining 1,007 steps increases the unrounded value by more than 1, so each yields a new floor. Total: 505 + 1,007 = 1,512.

Question 25 · Answer C

Write v = 12k and r = 3k+6. From any one vertex count two-edge walks that finish at a different vertex: there are r(r−1). Each of the other v−1 vertices is reached by exactly c such walks, so c = r(r−1)/(v−1) must be an integer. For the five possible k the ratios are 72/11, 132/23, 210/35, 306/47 and 420/59. Only 210/35 is integral, giving k = 3.
